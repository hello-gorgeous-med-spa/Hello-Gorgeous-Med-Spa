Close the consult

1. Name the goal. “You’re here for ___ (lines / scars / sun / neck). Solaria is the CO₂ for that — not a lunchtime facial.”
2. Name the cost of skipping. “Creams and facials cannot make this kind of column of new skin. If you want a real reset, this is the tool.”
3. Name downtime like a grown-up. Light / moderate / deeper from the client sheet. Ask: “What date can you hide for 5–7 days?” If they can’t, we go lighter or we wait.
4. Price with a fence. “Face is $599 this fall with serum. Neck and chest are extra because we treat them more gently and they take time. I’ll quote those before you book.”
5. Ask for the book. “Want me to hold a date that matches your downtime window?” Then Square /ops book — don’t leave it as ‘think about it’ if they’re a yes.

Before they arrive — confirm on the call

  • Candidate? History, goals, realistic downtime
  • Antiviral 1–2 days prior if indicated
  • No sun / tan / beds · SPF 50+ if they must be outside
  • Bring ointment + aftercare kit supplies
  • Clean, product-free skin
  • Button-down for face/neck/chest
  • No contacts
  • Ride home if anesthesia / sedation planned

Test spots

  • Several energy levels, smallest spot that still shows the expected response
  • Watch healing — adjust parameters from that, not from ego
  • Repeat a test spot whenever parameters change
  • To client: “We may test a tiny area so your full treatment is mapped to you.”

Day of — sequence

  • Antibacterial cleanse → 70% IPA
  • Photos before. Schedule follow-up photos.
  • Numbing ≤ 45–60 min. Sensitive skin: lower % + shorter time. Any reaction → stop.
  • Some need nerve block / injectable for higher energy
  • Pro-Nox / cold air OK with the comfort plan
  • Laser + smoke evacuator + cooling. Plume can contain live tissue.
  • Eyelids: intraocular shields. Everyone: protective eyewear. Non-reflective shields.
  • Start low energy + density. Watch tissue. Energy first, then density.
  • Wanted look: erythema, edema, stippled gray fractional epidermolysis
  • Do not debride, wipe eschar, or put on a wound dressing that prolongs healing. Eschar is the biologic dressing.

Teach aftercare out loud

  • Ice/cold packs up to 20 min on / 20 off, q2h
  • Occlusive ointment (as selected) — faster healing
  • Head elevated, 2+ pillows
  • Pink, tight, itchy is expected. Don’t pick.
  • Gentle cleanser 2×/day when allowed, re-apply ointment
  • Normal routine only after re-epithelialization + they feel comfortable
  • Meds only as prescribed (pain / antiviral / antihistamine)
  • Sun: SPF 50+ and a hat for the first weeks

Series talk

Number of sessions = area size + their response + their tolerance. Spacing depends on type, aggressiveness, and how they healed last time. The clinician — not the specials flyer — sequences treatments.
