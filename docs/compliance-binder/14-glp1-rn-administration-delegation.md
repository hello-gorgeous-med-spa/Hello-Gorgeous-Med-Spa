## 2. What this standing order authorizes

After the ordering clinician has completed a documented good-faith exam (binder **09**) and written a **patient-specific order**, the authorized RN may:

1. Confirm identity, consent, and that the order is current.
2. Perform the pre-injection screen in binder **15**.
3. Prepare and administer **one** subcutaneous dose matching the order (compounded semaglutide, compounded tirzepatide, or a branded GLP-1 **named on the order**).
4. Observe the patient **15 minutes** after the first in-clinic dose of a new molecule or after any prior hypersensitivity.
5. Document lot, BUD, dose, site, and tolerance.
6. For anaphylaxis only: call **911** and give epinephrine if authorized in §8.

The RN may repeat weekly doses **only while the same written order remains in effect** (see §4). The RN may **not** escalate, reduce, skip to a higher tier, or switch semaglutide ↔ tirzepatide without a new order.

## 4. How long an order lasts

Default (Dr. Arora may tighten):

- **Starter / titration:** one dose level for up to **4 weekly in-clinic injections**, then a new order is required before any increase.
- **Stable maintenance:** up to **8 weekly injections** at the same mg dose, then clinician re-check.
- **Any** new red flag, pregnancy, missed two consecutive doses, or patient request to change dose → **stop and obtain a new order.**
- Missed dose: **do not double.** Contact Dr. Arora the same day.

Payment, membership, or “they already bought the vial” is **not** an order.

## 6. Administration standard

- Wash hands. Clean vial stopper and skin with alcohol; let dry.
- Route: **subcutaneous** only. Sites: abdomen (≥2 inches from umbilicus), anterior thigh, posterior upper arm. Rotate. Do not inject bruised, scarred, or infected skin.
- Needle: insulin syringe or pharmacy-supplied needle appropriate to viscosity; 90° into pinched SQ tissue unless the order says otherwise.
- Draw the ordered mg using the **pharmacy label concentration**. If the math is unclear, **do not guess — call Dr. Arora.**
- Do not recap used needles. Sharps in an approved container.
- Refrigerate per label (typically 36–46°F / 2–8°C). Do not freeze. Do not use past BUD.
- Teach delayed gastric emptying in one sentence: smaller meals, protein, call for persistent vomiting.

## 7. Hard stops for the RN (do not inject — call Dr. Arora)

Ask out loud. A blank is not a No.

- Pregnant, trying to conceive, or positive pregnancy test
- Personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC) or MEN 2 (if not already cleared **and** something new is reported)
- Prior anaphylaxis / angioedema to a GLP-1
- Severe abdominal pain, vomiting that prevents fluids, jaundice, or suspected pancreatitis today
- Patient wants a different dose or product than the order
- Vial unlabeled, expired, discolored, frozen, or not from the ordered pharmacy
- Patient under 21, or not in Illinois
- Order expired per §4

Breastfeeding: do not inject unless the chart has an **explicit clinician decision** for this patient.

---

## 8. Emergency — anaphylaxis

**Call 911 first.** Then:

- IM epinephrine **0.3 mg** (1:1000 / 1 mg/mL **0.3 mL**, or adult autoinjector) into the **anterolateral thigh**. May repeat **once** in 5–15 minutes if still in anaphylaxis and EMS has not taken over — **only if this box is initialed by Dr. Arora:** ☐ Authorized
- Position, airway, oxygen if available, stay with the patient.
- Notify Dr. Arora as soon as 911 is called.
- Do not rechallenge GLP-1. Document time, dose, site, response.

Mild nausea after injection is expected. It is **not** anaphylaxis. Red-flag pain, persistent vomiting, or breathing difficulty is not “push through.”
