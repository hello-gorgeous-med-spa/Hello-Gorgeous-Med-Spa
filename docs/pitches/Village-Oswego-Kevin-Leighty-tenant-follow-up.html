Re: EDIA follow-up — tenant without equity, and a request for other programs

Dear Kevin,

Thank you again for the time you gave me, and for walking me through the Economic Development Incentive Award. I left that conversation grateful — and I have sat with the application since then. I am writing now because I need to be honest about where I stand, and because I am hoping you can point me toward something that might actually fit.

After reading the program the way it is written, I do not believe this grant applies to me. I am a tenant at 74 West Washington. I do not own the building, and I have no equity in it. The work the award is designed to fund — sewer, walls, electrical in the walls, building-mounted signs — becomes part of the property. It stays with the landlord. I would be putting more of my own money into improvements I cannot take with me and cannot recoup if the lease ends. That is a hard thing to ask of a small-business owner who has already poured her savings into someone else’s real estate.

I have been on this street since 2017. This year I put about $500,000 of medical-grade InMode equipment into this suite. I hired a physician. I have a family nurse practitioner seeing patients here through the week. I plumbed a real med room. I hired downtown staff. All of that came out of my pocket. I did it because I wanted Oswego families to have this care in town, and because I believed this block was worth betting on. I still believe that. I also have to look at the numbers as a tenant, not as an owner.

Downtown Oswego is a small place. The walkable part of Washington Street is only a few blocks. In a market that small, a second practice offering the same kind of care, so close to my door, is not an abstract “competitive landscape.” It is something I feel every day. I am not writing this to ask the Village to take a side, and I am not angry at anyone for opening. I am just tired, and a little heartbroken. I built this practice for almost a decade. I invested at a medical standard. And in a downtown this compact, there is only so much room before it starts to feel like the street cannot hold both of us the way I hoped it could.

That is the part that keeps me up. I have already put more money into this location than most tenants ever will — equipment that is mine, payroll that is mine, a medical practice I cannot simply pack into a box. If I keep spending on a building I do not own, while sharing a very small downtown with direct competition, I may have to consider changing locations. I do not want that. Leaving Washington Street would feel like giving up on the clients who have walked through my door since 2017. But staying as a tenant, with this much capital already in the floor and no equity to show for it, is becoming harder to justify.

I am not withdrawing my love for this town, and I am not asking you to change the EDIA rules for me. I understand why a program built around permanent building improvements is written for property owners. I am asking a different question: are there other Village, County, or partner programs I can apply for as a tenant — something meant for an operator who has already invested heavily, who is creating downtown jobs, and who is trying to stay, but who cannot put matching cash into a building she does not own?

If there is a tenant-facing grant, a façade program that can run through the property owner, a revolving loan I could carry on equipment I already purchased, Business Concierge help finding a longer lease or a different downtown suite, or any other door I have not seen — I will do the homework. I just need to know what is actually open to someone in my position.

Kevin, I would rather stay. I have already bet my savings, my licenses, and my next chapter of school on this street. I am writing because I do not want the next decision I make to be a quiet move out of downtown, when maybe there is a program that could help me stay and keep serving Oswego from here.

Thank you for reading this in the spirit I mean it — not as a complaint, but as a woman who is still trying, and who is asking for the right door.

With appreciation and respect,

Danielle Alcala
