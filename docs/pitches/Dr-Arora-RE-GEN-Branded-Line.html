What “white-label pharmacy” means here

How it works
Who compounds A licensed US 503A and/or 503B compounding pharmacy. They make the sterile medication. We do not become a pharmacy.
Who prescribes Ryan writes a patient-specific order after consult. You oversee the practice. Danielle does not prescribe.
What the patient sees RE GEN branding on the kit / outer pack / patient-facing materials. Professional, ours, consistent across every referred spa.
What the law still requires The pharmacy remains on the Rx label as required (compounder, BUD, Rx info). We are not hiding the source. We are owning the brand.
Who the patient belongs to Hello Gorgeous / RE GEN. Our consents. Our NP. Our pharmacy account. Referring spas never stock, sell, or inject the vials.

Why this matters with you as Medical Director

What it gives the practice

  • One formulary you can review and stand behind
  • One primary compounding partner — quality packet, not a rotating catalog of random stock
  • Patients (and referring spas) see a branded medical line, not a gray-market vial story
  • The moat stays with us: brand, patient, refill cadence — not the spa, not the pharmacy’s consumer name

What it is not

  • Not FDA-approved “RE GEN drugs.” Most peptides remain compounded, off-label, NP-directed
  • Not your name sold as a celebrity endorsement on a box
  • Not other spas’ logos on our vials
  • Not a software license, EHR project, or five white-label go-lives
  • Not day spas refrigerating medication
